User Guide#

The Thermochemistry step has two approaches. The harmonic approximation calculates the vibrational frequencies of a molecule from its Hessian, and from them the ideal-gas, rigid-rotor, harmonic-oscillator thermodynamic functions at the requested temperatures and pressures. The isodesmic (CBH) approach with ATcT references gives the enthalpy of formation of a molecule at 0 K and 298.15 K from the connectivity-based hierarchy of error-cancelling reactions, with the reference species taken from the Active Thermochemical Tables.

How it works#

The step needs the energy and the Hessian (second derivatives) of the structure. By default they come from the flowchart’s Model Chemistry: put a Model Chemistry step before this one to choose the program and method (MOPAC PM6-ORG, xTB, an ORCA DFT model, an MLFF model, …). The program runs as a warm engine over MDI. When it provides an analytic Hessian, as ORCA does for Hartree-Fock and most density functionals, that is used; otherwise the Hessian is built from central finite differences of the gradients over the same connection, or as separate calculations where the job runs its tasks. The step reports which route it took. The engine is started with the environment the program’s own runs get: for a program installed with environment modules (installation = modules in its .ini file, as on many clusters) its modules are loaded first.

The alternative, “Energies and gradients from: subflowchart”, keeps a subflowchart inside the step holding the engine (a MOPAC, ORCA, Gaussian, Psi4, LAMMPS or MLFF energy step, for example). The subflowchart runs once for the undisplaced structure and once for each displacement of each atom in ±x, ±y and ±z (6N+1 runs). The engine step must pass its energy and gradients back as JSON: in its Results tab, store energy and gradients as JSON. Without these the step stops with “There are no energy and gradients in properties.json”. Use this route for calculations an MDI engine cannot do. A flowchart made before the Model Chemistry route existed, with a subflowchart and no Model Chemistry, still runs as before.

The frequencies come from the Hessian through ASE. The six (five for a linear molecule) nominally zero translations and rotations are reported so that the quality of the gradients can be judged, and imaginary modes are excluded from the thermodynamic functions: by default the step perceives how many there are, or you can declare the structure a transition state or give the number.

Parameters#

Energies and gradients from

The flowchart’s Model Chemistry (default) or this step’s subflowchart.

Energies and Hessian via

automatic (default for the harmonic approach): the warm MDI engine when the program has an analytic Hessian or no task route, else finite differences as separate tasks where the job runs them; warm engine over MDI; or separate tasks. The CBH approach uses the engine by default, since it optimizes each species over it.

Step size

The finite-difference displacement, 0.01 Å by default; not used when the program provides the analytic Hessian.

Number of imaginary modes

perceive (default), transition state (one), or an integer.

Temperatures and pressures

Lists in the usual SEAMM form, e.g. 200, 250, 298.15, 300:1000:50 K and 1.0 bar.

Spin multiplicity

Taken from the system by default. It contributes R ln(2S+1) to the entropy.

Symmetry number

The rotational symmetry number σ, which enters the entropy as -R ln σ. By default (from system) it is perceived from the structure by testing rotation axes against the geometry; the step prints the number and the axes it found, e.g. “12 (4 C3, 3 C2 axes)” for methane. A geometry must be symmetric to within 0.1 Å for an axis to be found, which optimized structures are. Give the number explicitly if the structure is deliberately distorted.

Enthalpies of formation: the CBH approach#

Choose “Approach: Isodesmic (CBH) with ATcT references”. The step builds the connectivity-based hierarchy (CBH) reactions of the molecule from its bonds, so the structure must have bonds (built from SMILES, or read from an SDF or MOL file). CBH-0 breaks the molecule into hydrides of its atoms, CBH-1 into its bonds, CBH-2 keeps each atom with all its neighbours, CBH-3 each bond with its neighbours and CBH-4 each atom with two shells of neighbours: the higher the rung, the more of the chemical environment, and of the error of the method, is the same on both sides of the reaction. The reaction enthalpy is calculated with the engine for the target and every reference species, and the enthalpy of formation of the target follows from it and the ATcT values of the reference species, with the 95 % uncertainty of the ATcT part (using the ATcT covariances when they are cached). If the target itself is in ATcT the difference is reported too.

Radicals and ions are handled: every fragment containing the radical or charged atom keeps that spin or charge, and the ATcT entry is selected with the same charge and multiplicity. The reactions follow from the SMILES of the structure, so the system’s charge and spin multiplicity must agree with it; the step stops if they do not. Build a radical from its SMILES ([CH2]C for the ethyl radical), which sets both.

The ATcT tables are not distributed with SEAMM: install a version first with seamm-thermochemistry-atct download (see the seamm_thermochemistry documentation). A rung is usable only when all its reference species are in ATcT, and the step reports the rungs it could not use and why; the result stored is the highest usable rung, and every rung goes into the table CBH.

CBH rungs

all usable (default), or a list such as 1, 2.

Keep whole

Groups kept as single units when the reactions are built, so that they are never broken: by default aromatic ring systems, 3- and 4-membered rings and charge-separated or hypervalent groups (nitro, sulfonyl, phosphoryl, PF6-, …). none builds the plain hierarchy.

Species energies

finite-difference harmonic (default): each species is built from its SMILES as a system named CBH:<SMILES>, optimized with the engine and its Hessian calculated, giving H(0 K) = E + ZPE and H(298.15 K). The optimization convergence and maximum steps, and the finite-difference step size, are as for the harmonic approach. Or existing results (run nothing): the energies are read from properties already stored on those systems, named by the three property patterns (energy, ZPE and enthalpy; each glob must match exactly one property), for example from an earlier job that computed the fragments at a higher level.

Fragments start from, Target starts from

Each fragment built from its SMILES starts, by default, from the lowest MMFF94 conformer of 20 RDKit embeddings (an n-alkane all-anti, ethanol anti), as CBH-ANL starts every species from its lowest rotamer; a single embedding of a long chain is often gauche. The target is kept as given unless told to be rebuilt the same way. The chosen chain dihedrals are printed.

Thermal corrections

harmonic (default), or none when the engine’s energy is already an enthalpy of formation at 298.15 K, as for MOPAC and other NDDO methods: then no Hessian is calculated and only the 298.15 K result is available. Note that with NDDO methods CBH-0 is of no use, since their H2 is far from experiment.

Reference values

ATcT for every reference species; laddered: a CBH-ANL reference set built with seamm-thermochemistry-ladder (ATcT only for the CBH-0 species, every other species from its own lower-rung reaction at a high level), named in Ladder; or ATcT below the threshold, else laddered. With a ladder the uncertainty is the ATcT part through the ladder’s coefficients on the anchors (cᵀΣc with the covariance) plus the ladder’s measured method term, reported separately. Validate laddered results at 0 K: ATcT’s 298.15 K values are conformer averages.

Fragment store

Species already in the fragment-energy store at this model chemistry (seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest promotes them there from finished jobs) are taken from it instead of being computed, and serve as the fallback in “existing results” mode. The species table says where each value came from; a record without a settings fingerprint is “unverified” and the output says so. When the engine provides them (ORCA), the settings fingerprints of each species’ calculations are stored with its energies, so the harvest files verified records.

ATcT version, directory, covariances

The version of the Thermochemical Network (latest cached by default), where the versions are kept, and whether to fetch missing covariance blocks from atct.anl.gov (cached only by default: a compute node usually has no network, and the site may refuse the request; cache the blocks first with seamm-thermochemistry-atct covariance).

The results that can be stored are the enthalpy of formation at 0 K and at 298.15 K, its 95 % reference uncertainty, the rung and the reaction used, the ATcT version and, when the target is in ATcT, its values. They are also stored as the properties DfH(0 K), DfH(298.15 K) and DfH uncertainty (95%) of the configuration.

Every species the step computes, the target and each fragment, is written as it was computed (optimized, if optimization is on) to <SMILES>.sdf in the step’s directory, and all of them together to species.sdf, with the SMILES, the role (target or fragment), the model chemistry, E, ZPE, H(0 K), H(298.15 K) and the number of imaginary modes as SDF data fields. Open them to check that each conformation is the one intended; the output also lists the heavy-atom dihedrals of the final structure next to those of the starting one. The files also hold the properties stored on the structure, and can be read back with Read Structure, for example to restart a species from its computed geometry. Slashes in a SMILES (double-bond stereo) become _ and - in the file name. Species taken from the fragment store or from existing results are not written, since their structures are not computed here.

Results#

The step prints the ZPE, the electronic energy E of the undisplaced structure, H(0 K) = E + ZPE, and a table of the thermal corrections U-E, H-E and G-E (each including the ZPE, in kJ/mol) and the entropy S (J/mol/K) at every temperature and pressure.

The results that can be stored in variables, tables or as properties of the configuration are the frequencies, the symmetry number, the number of saddle modes and the transition-state frequency, the ZPE, the total (electronic) energy, H(0 K), and U, H, S and G. The last four are lists over the pressures and then the temperatures, in the same order as the stored P and T lists. The properties are named with the Model Chemistry’s level, for example H#Thermochemistry#MOPAC:SQM@PM6-ORG or S#Thermochemistry#ORCA:DFT@B3LYP/bse:def2-TZVP, or with the subflowchart engine’s model, e.g. H#Thermochemistry#PM6-ORG.
