User Guide#

The Thermochemistry step has two approaches. The harmonic approximation calculates the vibrational frequencies of a molecule from its Hessian, and from them the ideal-gas, rigid-rotor, harmonic-oscillator thermodynamic functions at the requested temperatures and pressures. The isodesmic (CBH) approach with ATcT references gives the enthalpy of formation of a molecule at 0 K and 298.15 K from the connectivity-based hierarchy of error-cancelling reactions, with the reference species taken from the Active Thermochemical Tables.

How it works#

The step needs the energy and the Hessian (second derivatives) of the structure. By default they come from the flowchart’s Model Chemistry: put a Model Chemistry step before this one to choose the program and method (MOPAC PM6-ORG, xTB, an ORCA DFT model, an MLFF model, …). The program runs as a warm engine over MDI. When it provides an analytic Hessian, as ORCA does for Hartree-Fock and most density functionals, that is used; otherwise the Hessian is built from central finite differences of the gradients over the same connection, or as separate calculations where the job runs its tasks. The step reports which route it took. The engine is started with the environment the program’s own runs get: for a program installed with environment modules (installation = modules in its .ini file, as on many clusters) its modules are loaded first.

The alternative, “Energies and gradients from: subflowchart”, keeps a subflowchart inside the step holding the engine (a MOPAC, ORCA, Gaussian, Psi4, LAMMPS or MLFF energy step, for example). The subflowchart runs once for the undisplaced structure and once for each displacement of each atom in ±x, ±y and ±z (6N+1 runs). The engine step must pass its energy and gradients back as JSON: in its Results tab, store energy and gradients as JSON. Without these the step stops with “There are no energy and gradients in properties.json”. Use this route for calculations an MDI engine cannot do. A flowchart made before the Model Chemistry route existed, with a subflowchart and no Model Chemistry, still runs as before.

The frequencies come from the Hessian through ASE. The six (five for a linear molecule) nominally zero translations and rotations are reported so that the quality of the gradients can be judged, and imaginary modes are excluded from the thermodynamic functions: by default the step perceives how many there are, or you can declare the structure a transition state or give the number.

Parameters#

Energies and gradients from

The flowchart’s Model Chemistry (default) or this step’s subflowchart.

Energies and Hessian via

automatic (default for the harmonic approach): the warm MDI engine when the program has an analytic Hessian or no task route, else finite differences as separate tasks where the job runs them; warm engine over MDI; or separate tasks. The CBH approach uses the engine by default, since it optimizes each species over it.

Step size

The finite-difference displacement, 0.01 Å by default; not used when the program provides the analytic Hessian.

Number of imaginary modes

perceive (default), transition state (one), or an integer.

Temperatures and pressures

Lists in the usual SEAMM form, e.g. 200, 250, 298.15, 300:1000:50 K and 1.0 bar.

Spin multiplicity

Taken from the system by default. It contributes R ln(2S+1) to the entropy.

Symmetry number

The rotational symmetry number σ, which enters the entropy as -R ln σ. By default (from system) it is perceived from the structure by testing rotation axes against the geometry; the step prints the number and the axes it found, e.g. “12 (4 C3, 3 C2 axes)” for methane. A geometry must be symmetric to within 0.1 Å for an axis to be found, which optimized structures are. Give the number explicitly if the structure is deliberately distorted.

Enthalpies of formation: the CBH approach#

Choose “Approach: Isodesmic (CBH) with ATcT references”. The step builds the connectivity-based hierarchy (CBH) reactions of the molecule from its bonds, so the structure must have bonds (built from SMILES, or read from an SDF or MOL file). CBH-0 breaks the molecule into hydrides of its atoms, CBH-1 into its bonds, CBH-2 keeps each atom with all its neighbours, CBH-3 each bond with its neighbours and CBH-4 each atom with two shells of neighbours: the higher the rung, the more of the chemical environment, and of the error of the method, is the same on both sides of the reaction. The reaction enthalpy is calculated with the engine for the target and every reference species, and the enthalpy of formation of the target follows from it and the ATcT values of the reference species, with the 95 % uncertainty of the ATcT part (using the ATcT covariances, fetched from atct.anl.gov when they are not cached). If the target itself is in ATcT the difference is reported too.

Radicals and ions are handled: every fragment containing the radical or charged atom keeps that spin or charge, and the ATcT entry is selected with the same charge and multiplicity. The reactions follow from the SMILES of the structure, so the system’s charge must agree with it, and so must its number of electrons: the step stops if they do not. Build a radical from its SMILES ([CH2]C for the ethyl radical), which sets both. The structure’s spin multiplicity may be lower than the unpaired electrons the SMILES shows: SiCl2 and BH are singlets that a SMILES writes with two unpaired electrons. The step then uses the structure’s multiplicity and says so, noting whether it is the known ground state. A fragment takes the spin multiplicity of its ground state: its unpaired electrons + 1, except the singlets that a SMILES writes with two unpaired electrons (SiH2, SiCl2, CF2, BH, …) and the triplets it writes closed-shell (O2, OCCO, cyclic C3, …; seamm_thermochemistry’s cbh.ground_state_multiplicity). The target keeps its structure’s multiplicity, and the step says so when that is not the ground state.

The ATcT tables are not distributed with SEAMM: the seamm-thermochemistry installer downloads the current version, and a calculation that finds none installed downloads it then (see the seamm_thermochemistry documentation; a newer version is added only on purpose, with seamm-thermochemistry-atct download). A rung is usable only when all its reference species are in ATcT, and the step reports the rungs it could not use and why; the result stored is the highest usable rung, and every rung goes into the table CBH. When no rung is usable but the target is itself in ATcT (methane, benzene, the methyl radical, CO: every rung reproduces the target), its ATcT value is the result, labelled “target is a reference species”, and nothing is computed. The target is found in ATcT also when ATcT writes it as another resonance form: SiO from a structure is [O+]#[Si-] but ATcT’s is [Si]=O, and OBrO is [O][Br+][O-] against [O]Br[O]. The match ignores bond orders, formal charges and radical centres but keeps the atoms, their connections and hydrogens, and the total charge, so isomers such as HCN and HNC stay apart; it is used only when exactly one ATcT species matches, and the output names the ATcT SMILES it matched. The spread of the enthalpies of formation over the rungs from CBH-1 up is reported and stored as an indicator of the uncertainty: a large spread points to trouble with the method or with the reference values.

Calculate

enthalpy of formation (default) runs the CBH reactions as described here. the species only (for the fragment store) computes only the structure itself, exactly as a fragment of a reaction: optimized and its harmonic frequencies at the geometry level, then its energy at the energy level, with the properties and settings fingerprints that seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest files in the store. It runs no reactions and needs no ATcT entry, so it can add any species to the store, for example to recompute stored species after a change of the settings that the fingerprints record. The species keeps the structure’s own name; harvest it with --species-map or --all-systems (or name the systems CBH:<SMILES>). Only the controls that apply are shown, and the ZPE is stored unscaled, as for every species.

The presets compute every species with ORCA, so they need no Model Chemistry step before this one, and scale the ZPEs by 0.9899. quick and default are the composite recipes SEAMM:CMP@r2SCAN-3c-HO and SEAMM:CMP@revDSD-r2SCAN-3c-HO when the Composite step (composite-step) is installed, and the results record the recipe, its version and its fingerprint; the levels and the numbers are the same either way. A composite recipe may also be the flowchart’s model chemistry with custom, for the CBH approach only, or the energy level. A recipe that combines several calculations runs each component as its own batch on every species; each component is looked up on its own in the job and in the fragment store, and stored as its own energy, never the combination. The species table names how each component was obtained, and a species whose component fails stops the step with that component named.

Every energy the step computes at the energy level, a component or not, is stored with its parts (HF, correlation, triples, …) when the program reports them. A recipe that uses parts takes them from the job or the store with the energy; a stored energy without parts is computed again, and the store then keeps its parts.

Each preset uses the highest rung available, at most CBH-2: CBH-3 is no more accurate (its error is dominated by the reference values), and Include CBH-3 adds it only to the spread between the rungs. One r2SCAN-3c geometry and frequency calculation serves every preset, so a species stored in the fragment store at one preset is not recomputed for another; only its energy is.

On 46 molecules against ATcT or vetted reference values at CBH-2, default has an RMS error of about 2.1 kJ/mol and quick about 2.2, but where CBH-2 is not available quick is much weaker: several kJ/mol at CBH-1, and 7 kJ/mol MAE on small aromatics, whose rings are kept whole so that CBH-2 is trivial. quick therefore warns when the highest rung is below CBH-2, and suggests default.

high takes the energies from CCSD(T)-F12, about 1 kJ/mol, chosen for each target:

  • canonical CCSD(T)-F12D/RI with cc-pVTZ-F12 when the target has at most 546 basis functions (Largest canonical F12 (basis functions); 546 fits in 96 GB of memory, and larger calculations need 200-330 GB);

  • otherwise DLPNO-CCSD(T)-F12D (TightPNO, TCutPNO 1e-8), but only for closed-shell molecules of H, C, N, O and F with no multiple or aromatic bonds and no two bonded fluorinated carbons, as validated;

  • otherwise no high result, with the reason, and the default result instead.

The fragments follow the target’s choice, so canonical and DLPNO energies are never mixed in one reaction. ORCA has cc-pVTZ-F12 for H to Ar only. high needs orca-step 2026.11.11.1 or later.

ZPE scale factor

from the table (default): the factor for the geometry level, with its source, which the output and the results give (r2SCAN-3c 0.9899, fitted to small molecules; PBE0/def2-TZVP 0.975, a value for another basis; B2PLYP-D3/cc-pVTZ 0.9863). A level not in the table is not scaled, with a warning. Or a number.

CBH rungs

all usable (default for custom), or a list such as 1, 2.

Keep whole

Groups kept as single units when the reactions are built, so that they are never broken: by default aromatic ring systems, 3- and 4-membered rings and charge-separated or hypervalent groups (nitro, sulfonyl, phosphoryl, PF6-, …). none builds the plain hierarchy.

Aromatic ring systems of any size and with any heteroatoms are kept whole, fused rings merged into one unit; non-aromatic 5- and 6-membered rings (cyclopentane, cyclohexane, THF, EC, FEC, …) are opened like chains. This default is the most accurate: on 14 molecules whose reactions differ either way, the mean absolute error against reference values was 0.9 kJ/mol kept whole against 2.0 opened with F12 energies, 2.2-2.3 against 2.9-3.5 with revDSD, and 6.0 against 10.8 with PBE0-D4. Opening a 3-membered ring leaves its strain to the method (methylcyclopropane: 0.1 against 5.0 kJ/mol with F12), and opening benzene into polyene fragments at CBH-3 is 3 to 6 kJ/mol off in every method tried.

Species energies

finite-difference harmonic (default): each species is built from its SMILES as a system named CBH:<SMILES>, optimized with the engine and its Hessian calculated, giving H(0 K) = E + ZPE and H(298.15 K). The optimization convergence and maximum steps, and the finite-difference step size, are as for the harmonic approach. Or existing results (run nothing): the energies are read from properties already stored on those systems, named by the three property patterns (energy, ZPE and enthalpy; each glob must match exactly one property), for example from an earlier job that computed the fragments at a higher level.

Energies at

the geometry level (default): the Model Chemistry gives the energies as well as the geometries, frequencies, ZPE and thermal corrections. Or another model chemistry, for example ORCA:DFT@REVDSD-PBEP86-D4_2021/def2-TZVPP or ORCA:QC@CCSD(T)-F12/cc-pVTZ-F12: its single point on each species’ geometry replaces the energy, and each species’ enthalpy becomes

H(T) = E(energy level) + [H(T) - E](geometry level)

so that a costly energy is used where it matters, on cheaper geometries and frequencies. The target’s single point runs first; the fragments’ then run as one batch of separate calculations, which can go to a cluster queue and can use methods that have no gradients (F12, for instance). This needs the computed species with harmonic thermal corrections. The species table shows both energies, and the results record both levels.

With the fragment store in use, a fragment whose geometry and frequencies at the geometry level are in the store is not recomputed, and one whose energy at the energy level, with the same settings, is stored on that geometry is not run again. The energy-level energies, their settings fingerprints and the geometry each ran on are stored on the species, so that seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest files them in the store’s geometry and energy tables.

Fragments start from, Target starts from

Each fragment built from its SMILES starts, by default, from the lowest MMFF94 conformer of 20 RDKit embeddings (an n-alkane all-anti, ethanol anti), as CBH-ANL starts every species from its lowest rotamer; a single embedding of a long chain is often gauche. The target is kept as given unless told to be rebuilt the same way. The chosen chain dihedrals are printed.

Thermal corrections

harmonic (default), or none when the engine’s energy is already an enthalpy of formation at 298.15 K, as for MOPAC and other NDDO methods: then no Hessian is calculated and only the 298.15 K result is available. Note that with NDDO methods CBH-0 is of no use, since their H2 is far from experiment.

Reference values

ATcT for every reference species; laddered: a CBH-ANL reference set built with seamm-thermochemistry-ladder (ATcT only for the CBH-0 species, every other species from its own lower-rung reaction at a high level), named in Ladder; or ATcT below the threshold, else laddered. With a ladder the uncertainty is the ATcT part through the ladder’s coefficients on the anchors (cᵀΣc with the covariance) plus the ladder’s measured method term, reported separately. Validate laddered results at 0 K: ATcT’s 298.15 K values are conformer averages.

Fragment store

use and add what is computed (default), use only or ignore. Species already in the fragment-energy store at this model chemistry are taken from it instead of being computed, and serve as the fallback in “existing results” mode. With use and add what is computed the species the step computes are added to the store at once, so later iterations of a Loop and later jobs find them; the output lists what was added and anything refused (a structure that rearranged while it was optimized, one with an imaginary mode). They are added only when the store is on a local disk of the machine running the job. Otherwise the job is marked, and is harvested into the store when it returns to its JobServer (or by hand with seamm-thermochemistry-fragments harvest).

The store is read in two layers: the installation’s own (local) store first, then SEAMM’s official one when it is installed. The species table says “store (official <version>)” for a value from the official store, and names both when an energy and its geometry come from different stores; the output notes any value on which the two disagree. Only the local store is ever written. Fragment store file names the local store. The species table says where each value came from; a record without a settings fingerprint is “unverified” and the output says so. When the engine provides them (ORCA), the settings fingerprints of each species’ calculations are stored with its energies, so the harvest files verified records.

Before the store, a fragment already computed in the same job – by an earlier iteration of a Loop over many targets, say – is taken from the job’s own database (“job” in the species table), when it was computed with the same settings and has no imaginary mode; an energy-level single point is reused only on the geometry it ran on. Each species is looked up with the fingerprints expected for it, not the target’s: ORCA uses exact exchange (NoCOSX) for atoms and for Na, Mg, Zn, B and P, so the methane fragment of a boron or phosphorus target has a different fingerprint from the target’s. If a computed fingerprint is not the one predicted, the output says so; the species is still used.

ATcT version, directory, covariances

The version of the Thermochemical Network (latest cached by default), where the versions are kept, and whether to fetch missing covariance blocks from atct.anl.gov (fetch if needed, the default) or use only those cached (cached only). A block that is missing, or that cannot be fetched (a compute node often has no network, and the site may refuse the request; the step stops trying after the first failure), leaves the reference uncertainty in quadrature, without the correlations. The result’s uncertainty method says which, and seamm-thermochemistry-atct covariance caches the blocks ahead of time.

The results that can be stored are the enthalpy of formation at 0 K and at 298.15 K, its 95 % reference uncertainty and how it was obtained, the rung and the reaction used, the ATcT version and, when the target is in ATcT, its values. They are also stored as the properties DfH(0 K), DfH(298.15 K) and DfH uncertainty (95%) of the configuration.

Every species the step computes, the target and each fragment, is written as it was computed (optimized, if optimization is on) to <SMILES>.sdf in the step’s directory, and all of them together to species.sdf, with the SMILES, the role (target or fragment), the model chemistry, E, ZPE, H(0 K), H(298.15 K) and the number of imaginary modes as SDF data fields. Open them to check that each conformation is the one intended; the output also lists the heavy-atom dihedrals of the final structure next to those of the starting one. The files also hold the properties stored on the structure, and can be read back with Read Structure, for example to restart a species from its computed geometry. Slashes in a SMILES (double-bond stereo) become _ and - in the file name. Species taken from the fragment store or from existing results are not written, since their structures are not computed here.

Results#

The step prints the ZPE, the electronic energy E of the undisplaced structure, H(0 K) = E + ZPE, and a table of the thermal corrections U-E, H-E and G-E (each including the ZPE, in kJ/mol) and the entropy S (J/mol/K) at every temperature and pressure.

The results that can be stored in variables, tables or as properties of the configuration are the frequencies, the symmetry number, the number of saddle modes and the transition-state frequency, the ZPE, the total (electronic) energy, H(0 K), and U, H, S and G. The last four are lists over the pressures and then the temperatures, in the same order as the stored P and T lists. The properties are named with the Model Chemistry’s level, for example H#Thermochemistry#MOPAC:SQM@PM6-ORG or S#Thermochemistry#ORCA:DFT@B3LYP/bse:def2-TZVP, or with the subflowchart engine’s model, e.g. H#Thermochemistry#PM6-ORG.
